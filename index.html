Ran it? Do this.

No technical skills needed. Use your phone or another computer.

First

Disconnect the Mac

  • Turn off Wi-Fi.
  • Stop using the Mac.
Second

Move your crypto

  • Set up a new wallet with a new recovery phrase.
  • Send everything to it.
Third

Lock your accounts

  • Change your email password first.
  • Choose "sign out of all devices".
  • Turn on two-step verification.
  • Tell your bank, and IT if it's a work Mac.
Fourth

Erase the Mac

  • Save documents and photos only.
  • System Settings › General › Transfer or Reset › Erase All Content and Settings.
  • Reinstall apps fresh.

Then watch for sign-in alerts, and ignore anyone offering to "recover" your money.

The evidence.

What I found in the sample, and where each finding came from.

  1. 01

    The paste hides a download

    The encoded block decodes to a short script. It records the page you were on, saves a hidden file in a temporary folder, runs it in the background, then clears the window and your history.

  2. 02

    It switches off the warning

    The download script picks the right build for your Mac, strips the "downloaded from the internet" flag, signs the program itself, starts it detached, and erases the last line of your shell history.

  3. 03

    It reports every step

    Both the script and the program send status messages to the attacker's server, so the operator sees exactly how far each infection got, including when macOS privacy settings block it.

  4. 04

    It counts what it took

    When collection finishes, it reports a tally by category. Everything it uploads is encrypted first, so the contents can't be read from the traffic.

  5. 05

    It goes after wallet apps

    The program carries code that modifies Exodus, Atomic Wallet, Ledger Live and Trezor Suite, plus a fake "restore wallet" screen. These are the labels it gives what it captures.

  6. 06

    It reaches into the keychain

    Built-in helpers unlock the macOS keychain and list what's stored in it, which points to it asking for or reusing your login password.

  7. 07

    It sets up to stay

    Its settings are readable inside the program: the Apple-looking name, a delay before it installs itself at login, and a separate long-running process that swaps crypto addresses.

Analyzed in an isolated lab. The malware was never run on a real Mac. Sample from an infection captured and published by Brad Duncan with SANS ISC (malware-traffic-analysis.net).
